## Exhibition statement

HSU, Che-jui | Exhibition statement

What remains of art after we leave the museum?

This solo exhibition brings together six works spanning 4×5 film photography, AI-generated imagery, performance art, sound art, and an open-source education project.

"Answer" and "The King of Examination" ask: Who decides the right answer? Who gets to speak?

"When I Grow Up" uses AI to imagine different lives, revealing how seemingly limitless possibilities remain shaped by cultural biases and expectations, and by representational imbalances between dominant and marginalized cultures in AI training data.

"Three Days Without Reading, a Face Grown Loathsome" feeds each AI-generated portrait into the next generation, cycling through distortion and clarity while drifting from the source despite repeated prompts to "preserve the original appearance."

"Picnolepsy" invites visitors to answer actual questions from Taiwan’s General Scholastic Ability Test. Through their correct and incorrect choices, they collectively create forked versions of "Rainy Night Flower" and "Longing for the Spring Breeze."

Even if an artist declares that “life has no standard answers,” students must still enter the exam room tomorrow and face the real consequences of their scores. In response, I created "Open Book Is Good," turning publicly available exam questions into a free, open-source self-study tool—putting practice materials, detailed explanations, and the means of making the tool into the public’s hands.

Students can use it, and anyone, anywhere in the world, can contribute changes, keeping its knowledge open to scrutiny and expansion.

From questions raised in the exhibition to forms of social participation we can improve together in everyday life, I hope art leaves behind more than reflection: a concrete practice that helps us face reality.
